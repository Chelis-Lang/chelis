A bracket literal is a `List` whatever its elements and wherever it stands. It becomes a tensor only as the argument of `to_tensor(...)`, or where its own binding (inline annotation or standalone `sig`) or its function's declared result, inline or in a `sig`, is a tensor type. An unannotated `xs = [1.0, 2.0]` is again a `List f32`; write `xs = to_tensor([1.0, 2.0])` for a tensor. A tensor parameter or a `cast` no longer converts a bare bracket literal: write `f(to_tensor([1.0, 2.0]))` and `cast(to_tensor([1.1, 2.2]), f64)`, where the unsuffixed elements take the parameter's or the target's dtype, so the cast binds `1.1` at `f64` instead of rounding it through `f32`. `chelis surf` prints a tensor-typed binding's literal bare, so its elements keep their declared dtype through a round trip. See [#3114](https://github.com/Chelis-Lang/chelis/issues/3114) and [#3080](https://github.com/Chelis-Lang/chelis/issues/3080).

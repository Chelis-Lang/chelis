A bracket literal is a `List` whatever its elements and wherever it stands. It becomes a tensor only through `to_tensor(...)`, or where its own binding (inline annotation or standalone `sig`) or its `def`'s declared result is a tensor type; that declaration gives its unsuffixed elements the element dtype, as before. A `def` with no inline result type also converts its bare bracket body under a standalone `sig`'s tensor result, and an inline `List[...]` result keeps a `List` body under any `sig`. An unannotated `xs = [1.0, 2.0]` is a `List f32`; write `xs = to_tensor([1.0, 2.0])` for a tensor. A tensor parameter or a `cast` no longer converts a bare bracket literal, so `f([1.1, 2.2])` and `cast([1.1, 2.2], f64)` are type errors whose diagnostic names a value-preserving fix. Their elements took the parameter's or the cast target's dtype, but `to_tensor` keeps each element's suffix or default: write `cast(to_tensor([1.1f64, 2.2f64]), f64)` or bind the literal under a declared tensor type to keep those values, since `cast(to_tensor([1.1, 2.2]), f64)` widens `f32` values. `chelis surf` prints a tensor-typed binding's literal bare, so its elements keep their declared dtype through a round trip. See [#3114](https://github.com/Chelis-Lang/chelis/issues/3114) and [#3080](https://github.com/Chelis-Lang/chelis/issues/3080).

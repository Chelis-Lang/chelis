The ownership rules around `drop` are now decided and enforced. A `drop` after an earlier ordinary consume, such as a consuming call argument or `realize`, stays legal: the earlier use receives the inserted copy, and [05-OP-67] no longer says a second consume is always rejected. A `drop` after a match on the value or a consuming closure capture stays rejected. A `drop` of a value that a previously created closure borrows is now `InvalidBorrow`, whether or not the closure is called again; capture a `copy` to drop the original. A tuple projection or field access moves its component out of the parent, so after `drop(p.0)` or `drop(r.f)` a use of `p` or `r` as a whole, or of the dropped component, is `UseAfterConsume`, while the other components stay usable and an ordinary consume such as `eat(p.0)` still leaves `p` usable. See [#3178](https://github.com/Chelis-Lang/chelis/issues/3178) and [#3181](https://github.com/Chelis-Lang/chelis/issues/3181).
